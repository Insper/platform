\ans{3} (a) $10^8/(30 \times 86\,400) \approx$ \final{39 writes/s}; $\times 100 \approx$ \final{3\,900 redirects/s}, peak \final{$\approx$ 12\,000/s}; $10^8 \times 60 =$ \final{6 billion records}, $\times 500$ B $=$ \final{3 TB}. (b) $62^6 \approx 5.7 \times 10^{10}$: enough in count, but tight and guessable; $62^7 \approx 3.5 \times 10^{12}$: \final{7 characters, 0.17\% used}. (c) $3\,858 \times 86\,400 \times 0.2 \times 500$ B $\approx$ \final{33 GB}: in a cache (1--2 nodes). (d) A range touches the shared counter \final{once per 10\,000 IDs} (no hot counter, no coordination per write); \final{sequential codes leak} volume and are enumerable, so scramble (multiply by a large odd constant mod $62^7$, or a Feistel cipher). (e) \texttt{POST /v1/urls} $\to$ 201 \{code, shortUrl\}; \texttt{GET /\{code\}} $\to$ \final{302} (404/410). A 301 is cached by browsers forever: you lose the click analytics and cannot expire or change the link.
\careful{choosing hash-and-truncate without handling collisions, or 301 ``because it is faster'' and losing every click after the first.}
\ans{4} (a) Fixed window: \final{20} (10 in each window, 20 in 20 ms). Token bucket: 10 at 0.99 s, then only 0.2 tokens refilled by 1.01 s: \final{10}. Sliding log: \final{10}. (A sliding counter allows about 11.) (b) Up to \final{$20 \times 10 = 200$ req/s, 20$\times$ the limit}. Fixes: \final{a shared counter} (Redis, atomic Lua script, hash tag per user) or \final{local buckets of limit/N} (here 0.5/s), or local plus periodic sync. (c) $10^6 \times 2 \times 100$ B $=$ \final{200 MB} vs $10^6 \times 600 \times 50$ B $=$ \final{30 GB}. (d) \final{Fail open} for browsing (a limiter outage must not take the API down; keep a local fallback bucket); \final{fail closed} for login, password reset and OTP (brute force).
\careful{the fixed-window boundary (2$\times$ the limit) and per-replica limits (N$\times$ the limit) are the two classic overshoots.}
\ans{5} (a) $10^9 \times 1\,100$ B $=$ \final{1.1 TB}; $1.1\,\text{TB}/64\,\text{GB} = 17.2 \to 18$ by memory; $2\,\text{M}/80\,\text{k} =$ \final{25 by throughput}: \final{25 primaries, 50 nodes}. Throughput, not memory, decides. (b) $2\,\text{M} \times 0.05 =$ \final{100\,000/s}; at 90\% \final{200\,000/s}. \final{Each point $=$ 20\,000 DB req/s.} Losing 1 of 25 nodes sends its 4\% of the traffic to the database: \final{+80\,000 misses/s}, a stampede unless you coalesce, fail over to the replica and warm up. (c) \texttt{mod N}: \final{80\%} of the keys move (a key stays only if $h \bmod 4 = h \bmod 5$); ring: \final{$\approx$ 20\%} (1/5). Virtual nodes spread each node's ownership in many arcs, so load is even and a failed node's keys spread over all the others.
\careful{sizing a cache cluster by memory only; at 2 M ops/s the operations per node set the count.}
\ans{6} (a) $2 \times 10^7$ posts/day $\approx$ \final{231/s}; $10^9$ reads/day $\approx$ \final{11\,600/s, 35\,000 at the peak}; \final{50:1}. (b) $231 \times 200 \approx$ \final{46\,000 inserts/s}; $10^8 \times 800 \times 8 \times 2 =$ \final{1.28 TB} of RAM; $2 \times 10^7 \times 1$ KB $\times 365 =$ \final{7.3 TB/year}. (d) \final{Hybrid}: push for normal authors; for the 1.7\% above the threshold, \final{pull at read time} (read the precomputed timeline, fetch the recent posts of the celebrities the user follows, merge and rank). Skip inactive users and rebuild their timeline on login.

\ans{7} (a) $300\,000/86\,400 \approx$ \final{3.5/s}; \final{600 in flight} at the peak; $300\,000 \times 0.001 =$ \final{300 double charges a day}.
