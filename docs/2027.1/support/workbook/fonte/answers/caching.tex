\ansclass{7}{Caching}
\ans{1} (a) $\ln 1\,000 / \ln 100\,000 = 3/5 =$ \final{$\approx$ 60\% of the reads} from 1\% of the catalog. (b) Event description: \final{cache} ($\approx$ 1 min, delete on write $+$ TTL 5 min). Seat map: \final{cache 1--2 s} (it is only displayed). Hold of A-12: \final{never}, it is a decision (SQL). Price at checkout: \final{never}, the order re-reads it from the database (the page may show a price cached for seconds). Session: \final{in Redis, but as its source of truth} (no eviction), not as a cache.
\careful{caching what you decide on. ``Cache what you display, not what you decide on.''}
\ans{2} (a) $t_\text{eff}$: \final{1.3, 0.58 and 0.51 ms}; database: \final{5\,000, 500 and 50 QPS}. (b) 95\%: $0.05 \times 40\,000 =$ \final{2\,000 QPS}; 99\%: \final{400 QPS}, five times less. Four points of hit ratio are a factor of 5 in the miss ratio, which is what the database feels.
\careful{reading the hit ratio linearly. Compute the misses: $(1-h)$ is the load that reaches the database, and the slow path is your p99.}
\ans{3} (a) $20\,000 \times 4.1$ KB $\approx$ \final{82 MB}. (b) $82 + 400 + 1\,000$ MB $\approx$ \final{1.5 GB}; with a replica \final{3 GB}; with 50\% headroom \final{$\approx$ 4.5 GB}: one small node and its replica. (c) $300\,000 / 100\,000 =$ \final{3 primaries} (each with a replica): \final{throughput, not memory, sizes the cluster}. Keep sessions on a separate instance without eviction.
\careful{sizing the cache for the whole dataset instead of the hot working set, or forgetting the replica and the headroom.}
\ans{4} (a) $rT = 60\,000$: \final{one miss every 60\,001 reads}. The cold event is read once per 10 minutes and its entry lives 60 s: \final{every read misses}, $h = 0$. Short TTLs hurt the long tail, not the hot keys. (b) The cache keeps \final{100 while the database says 120, until the TTL expires} (the TTL is the safety net). With delete, whoever runs last removes the key and the next read loads 120 from the database. (c) If the key is deleted inside the transaction, a reader can miss, read the \emph{old} committed row and put it back before the commit: the stale value returns. \final{Write, commit, then delete}.
\careful{updating the cache instead of deleting it, or deleting before the commit.}
\ans{5} (a) Little's Law: $20\,000 \times 0.2 =$ \final{4\,000 queries} hit the database at once; with coalescing, one query per replica: \final{20}. (b) All 5\,000 keys expire together at \final{10:00:00}, exactly when the on-sale starts: an \final{avalanche}. Fix: \final{TTL $= 600 +$ random(0, 120) s} (jitter) or refresh-ahead. (c) Each id is new, so every request misses and goes to the database (\final{penetration}). $9.6$ bits $\times 100\,000 \approx$ \final{120 KB}: a Bloom filter answers ``certainly not an event'' for 99\% of the bogus ids; add a short negative cache and the WAF.
\careful{a lock without an expiry (PX) for the rebuild: if the holder dies, everyone waits forever.}
\ans{6} (a) $2 \times 10^6 / 5 =$ \final{400\,000 reads/s}; $\times 4$ KB $= 1.6$ GB/s $\approx$ \final{13 Gbit/s, more than the 10 Gbit/s card}. (b) The key lives in one hash slot, on one node: \final{more nodes do not split a single key}. (c) L1: each replica asks Redis once per second, \final{$\approx$ 50 reads/s}; cost: up to 1 s of staleness and 50 copies to invalidate. Eight copies: \final{50\,000 reads/s each}; cost: 8 writes per update and a random suffix on every read.
\careful{scaling the cluster to fix a hot key.}
\ans{7} (a) A good plan: description \final{CDN/gateway seconds, L1 2 s, Redis 5 min, delete on write}; venue \final{1 h, versioned key}; price table \final{Redis 30 s (re-read at checkout)}; seat map \final{1 s or none}; ``seats left'' \final{TTL 2 s} (approximate counter); seat hold \final{never}. (b) \final{Short timeouts} on Redis (tens of ms), a \final{circuit breaker} that stops waiting for it, a \final{limit on misses} that may reach the database (e.g. 2\,000 QPS) and \final{load shedding} or the waiting room for the rest; never ``fall back to the database for everything''. (c) Client $\to$ CDN $\to$ gateway $\to$ app (L1) $\to$ Redis $\to$ SQL; writes go to SQL and, after commit, delete in Redis and publish an invalidation (pub/sub) to the L1s.
\careful{assuming the cache is optional once the database is sized for the misses: a cold or missing cache sends 50$\times$ the load.}
