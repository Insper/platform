\activity{3}{Distributed Systems}{distributed}{It is 10:00:00 and 2 million fans press ``Hold seat'' for 60\,000 seats, each to be sold exactly once. One fan, on a train, waits five seconds, sees ``Something went wrong, try again'' and presses again. Did the first request hold a seat? Is she about to hold two, or be charged twice? How can BoxOffice, running on many machines joined only by a network, sell each seat once and never charge her twice?}
\stages{\stDesign\ \stDeep}

\begin{know}
  \item A \textbf{distributed system} is a set of nodes that cooperate only by messages: no shared memory, no shared clock. If each of $N$ machines fails on a given day with probability $p$, some machine fails with probability $1 - (1-p)^N$.
  \item A caller \textbf{cannot tell slow from dead}: after a timeout the request may have been lost, processed with the reply lost, or still be running. Retried operations must be \textbf{idempotent}, $f(f(x)) = f(x)$: conditional writes (compare-and-set) or an idempotency key stored in the same transaction as the change. ``Exactly once'' is at-least-once delivery $+$ idempotent processing.
  \item \textbf{Replication}: single-leader (no write conflicts), multi-leader and leaderless (conflicts: last-writer-wins, version vectors, CRDTs, conflict-free replicated data types). Asynchronous followers lag: a read right after a write may be stale. \textbf{Read-your-writes}: read from the leader for a few seconds after a write, or wait for a version token.
  \item \textbf{CAP}: during a network partition, choose consistency (linearizability: refuse) or availability (answer, maybe stale). \textbf{PACELC}: else, without a partition, choose latency or consistency; a synchronous write pays one round trip to the replicas (1--2 ms across zones, 150--200 ms across continents).
  \item \textbf{Majorities}: majority $= \lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1$, tolerated failures $= \lfloor (N-1)/2 \rfloor$. Use 3 or 5 nodes across 3 zones. Raft elects one leader per term and commits an entry once a majority has it.
  \item \textbf{Quorums} in leaderless stores: with $N$ replicas, writes to $W$ and reads from $R$ overlap when $R + W > N$.
  \item A \textbf{lease} or lock expires; the storage must also check a \textbf{fencing token} that grows with every grant and reject tokens lower than the highest it has seen.
  \item \textbf{Across services} no 2PC (two-phase commit: blocks when the coordinator fails): a \textbf{saga} of local transactions with compensations; the successful charge is the pivot step.
  \item \textbf{No global clock}: clocks drift (20 ppm $\approx$ 1.7 s/day). Lamport clocks give $a \to b \Rightarrow L(a) < L(b)$ (receive: $\max(\text{local}, t) + 1$); vector clocks also detect concurrency. Never order events by wall clocks of different machines.
\end{know}

\begin{ex}{1}{Failure is normal}
Each machine of a fleet fails on a given day with probability $p = 0.1\%$, independently.
\items
  \item What is the probability that at least one machine fails on a given day with 3 machines? With 1\,000? With 3\,000?
  \item Use $(1-p)^N \approx e^{-pN}$ to check the 1\,000-machine case in your head.
  \item What does the result mean for how you design a service that runs on 3\,000 machines?
\end{enumerate}
\lines{4}
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}{1}{Safe to retry?}
\items
  \item Mark each operation as idempotent (I) or not (N):\\[.3em]
  {\small\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
    \texttt{GET /events/42} & \blank\hspace{1cm} \\
    \texttt{PUT /holds/A-12 \{"fan": 42\}} & \blank \\
    \texttt{DELETE /holds/A-12} & \blank \\
    \texttt{POST /orders} & \blank \\
    \texttt{UPDATE seats SET sold = sold + 1} & \blank \\
    \texttt{UPDATE seats SET status='HELD' WHERE id='A-12' AND status='FREE'} & \blank \\
  \end{tabular}}
  \item \texttt{order} asks \texttt{payment} to authorize R\$ 450 and gets no reply in 2 s. List the four things that may have happened. In which of them does a plain retry charge the fan twice?
\end{enumerate}
\lines{4}
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}{2}{The idempotency key}
The app creates a UUID when the fan taps ``Pay'' and sends it as \texttt{Idempotency-Key} on every attempt. \texttt{order} has a table \texttt{idempotency\_keys(key PRIMARY KEY, request\_hash, response)}.
\items
  \item Write, in order, the three statements of the transaction that creates order 981, so that a retry with the same key returns the first response and creates nothing.
  \item Why must the key be stored in the \emph{same} transaction as the order, and not in Redis after the commit?
  \item The reply \texttt{201 Created} is lost on the train and the app retries. Trace what happens. What does the fan get?
  \item A developer generates a new UUID on each retry ``to be safe''. What breaks?
  \item The same key is forwarded to the payment provider. Why? The provider keeps keys for 24 hours: what does that limit?
\end{enumerate}
\lines{6}
\end{ex}

\simulator{Retries need idempotency}{payment-system}{The provider becomes 4$\times$ slower at t = 60 s and retries after a timeout charge cards again. Turn on the idempotency key on the payment edge and watch the duplicate charges drop to zero.}

\begin{ex}{2}{The stale ``My tickets''}
The fan paid at 10:00:41 and saw ``Order 981 confirmed'', but ``My tickets'', read from an asynchronous replica about 300 ms later, was empty. Model the chance of a stale read as $P(\text{stale}) = e^{-t/\bar\ell}$, with $t$ the time since the write and $\bar\ell$ the mean replication lag.
\items
  \item Compute $P(\text{stale})$ at $t = 300$ ms for $\bar\ell = 20$ ms (a quiet day), 200 ms (busy) and 800 ms (the on-sale).
  \item Which guarantee is the fan missing? Give two ways to provide it without making every read go to the leader.
  \item The remaining-seat counter shows 120, then 340, then 95 on three refreshes. Which guarantee is broken, and how do you fix it?
  \item For each piece of BoxOffice data, choose the weakest model that is still correct: seats and holds, payments, ``My tickets'', the remaining-seat counter, the catalog, ``people interested'' counters.
\end{enumerate}
\lines{6}
\end{ex}

\simulator{Replicas and lag}{db-replicas}{Add read replicas and watch reads spread while the primary keeps every write. Raise the write share to 30\%: the replication lag grows, and so does the chance of the stale read you just computed.}

\begin{ex}{2}{Majorities, quorums and fences}
\items
  \item Fill in the majority and the failures tolerated for $N = 3, 4, 5$ and 7. Why is a 4-node cluster a bad idea?
  \item Five etcd nodes are spread over 3 zones as 2$+$2$+$1 or as 3$+$1$+$1. In each layout, one zone with the most nodes dies. Can the cluster still accept writes?
  \item Three nodes of 99\% availability each: what is the availability of the majority (2 of 3 up)? Use $a^3 + 3a^2(1-a)$.
  \item A Cassandra-style store has $N = 3$. Which pairs $(W, R)$ guarantee that a read sees the last acknowledged write: (1, 1), (2, 2), (3, 1), (1, 3), (2, 1)? Which pair would you pick, and what does it tolerate?
  \item Worker A holds the lock for the 09:55 reminder with fencing token 33, pauses 15 s in garbage collection and its lease expires; worker B gets token 34 and writes. A wakes up and writes. What does the storage do, and why?
\end{enumerate}
\lines{6}
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}{2}{Whose clock?}
Bruno releases seat A-12 at real time 10:00:00.040 on \texttt{inventory-1}, whose clock is 50 ms ahead. Carla holds A-12 at real time 10:00:00.060 on \texttt{inventory-2}, whose clock is right. The store resolves conflicts by last-writer-wins on the timestamps.
\items
  \item Which write wins, and what happens to Carla?
  \item A clock drifts 20 ppm (parts per million). How far off is it after one day without NTP? And a 40 ppm clock after 10 minutes?
  \item Lamport clocks: \texttt{order} creates order 981 ($L = 1$) and sends \texttt{OrderPaid} with $L = 2$ to the \texttt{inventory} consumer, whose clock is 0. The consumer then sends \texttt{TicketIssued} to \texttt{notification}, whose clock is 1 after an unrelated local event. Compute the consumer's clock on receipt, the timestamp of \texttt{TicketIssued} and \texttt{notification}'s clock on receipt.
  \item Two replicas have vector clocks $[1, 1, 0]$ and $[1, 0, 1]$. Which came first?
  \item Propose two ways to order the hold and the release correctly.
\end{enumerate}
\lines{6}
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}{3}{Two regions and a checkout}
BoxOffice runs in São Paulo and Lisbon, each with a full copy of the data, and the link between them drops for 2 minutes during an on-sale.
\items
  \item For seats, payments, the catalog and like counters, choose CP or AP and say exactly what the fan sees during the partition.
  \item Without a partition, a checkout makes 5 sequential writes. How long do they take with synchronous replication inside a region (1--2 ms per write) and across the Atlantic ($\approx$ 200 ms per write)? What does PACELC tell you to do with seats?
  \item Design the checkout as a saga over \texttt{order}, \texttt{inventory} and \texttt{payment}: list the steps, the compensation of each, and the pivot step. The card is declined at the payment step: which compensations run, in which order?
  \item Why not a 2PC across the three services? Give two reasons.
\end{enumerate}
\lines{7}
\end{ex}

\simulator{Losing a region}{multi-region}{Build the second region and connect DNS to it. When the first region goes down at t = 60 s, watch errors last about detection plus the DNS TTL, and think of the writes that had not replicated yet.}
