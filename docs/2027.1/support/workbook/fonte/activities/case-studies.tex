\activity{16}{Case Studies}{case-studies}{It is 10:00 and BoxOffice opens the sale of a stadium show: two million fans, sixty thousand seats. Over the semester you built every piece needed to survive that morning. Now an interviewer gives you a one-sentence prompt, a blank whiteboard and 45 minutes. How do you turn the requirements into numbers and the numbers into a design (cache, queue, partition key, idempotency, admission control), and defend what breaks first?}
\stages{\stClarify\ \stEstimate\ \stDesign\ \stDeep}

\begin{know}
  \item \textbf{The 45 minutes}: clarify (5) $\to$ estimate (5) $\to$ API and data model (5--7) $\to$ high-level design (10) $\to$ deep dives (10--15) $\to$ bottlenecks, failures and trade-offs (5) $\to$ wrap-up (2--3). Broad before deep.
  \item \textbf{Every number ends in a decision}: QPS $=$ events/day $\div$ 86\,400 (1 M/day $\approx$ 12/s), peak $=$ average $\times$ 3 to 5, storage $=$ count $\times$ bytes $\times$ retention ($\times$ replication).
  \item \textbf{Little's Law} $L = \lambda W$ appears everywhere: admission rate $\lambda = L/W$, calls in flight $=$ rate $\times$ latency, active trips, concurrent streams.
  \item \textbf{Cache math}: database load $=$ ops $\times (1-h)$ for a hit ratio $h$. \textbf{ID space}: $62^7 \approx 3.5 \times 10^{12}$ codes of 7 base-62 characters.
  \item \textbf{Consistent hashing}: going from $N$ to $N+1$ nodes, \texttt{hash mod N} moves about $1 - 1/(N+1)$ of the keys, a ring about $1/(N+1)$; virtual nodes balance the load.
  \item \textbf{Exactly-once effect $=$ at-least-once $+$ idempotency}: idempotency keys, \texttt{SET NX} claims, event-id dedupe, conditional updates, unique constraints. \textbf{A timeout means unknown, not failed.}
  \item \textbf{Fan-out} on write (push), on read (pull) or hybrid with a celebrity threshold; spread bursts (campaigns, waiting rooms): turn a spike into a flow.
  \item \textbf{Strong consistency only where it matters}: seats, money, driver assignment. Feeds, counters, positions and analytics are eventual. Protect the one resource that cannot scale (hot rows, the payment provider) with \textbf{admission control}, not with autoscaling.
\end{know}

\begin{ex}{1}{Little's Law in five systems}
Compute the missing quantity and say what it sizes.
\items
  \item Ticketing: at most 10\,000 fans may shop at once and each takes 5 minutes. Admission rate?
  \item Payments: 300 payments/s at the peak, the provider answers in 2 s. Calls in flight?
  \item Ride sharing: 20 M trips a day of 20 minutes each. Trips per second and active trips?
  \item Video: 50 M daily viewers watch 30 minutes each. Concurrent streams on average?
  \item AI assistant: 39 LLM turns/s at the peak; a turn is 0.8 s to the first token, 300 tokens at 50 tokens/s, and 1 s of tool calls. Concurrent streams?
\end{enumerate}
\lines{5}
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}{2}{URL shortener in ten minutes}
``Design \texttt{bx.example/aB3x9Kq}'': 100 M new links a month, 100 redirects per link, kept 5 years, 500 B per record, redirect p99 $<$ 100 ms.
\items
  \item Writes/s, redirects/s (average and peak $\times$3), number of records and storage.
  \item Is 6 base-62 characters enough? And 7? What fraction of the 7-character space do you use in 5 years?
  \item The hot set is 20\% of a day's redirected records. How big is it, and where does it live?
  \item Codes come from a counter leased in ranges of 10\,000 and encoded in base 62. Why ranges, and why must the numbers be scrambled before encoding?
  \item Write the two endpoints. Redirect with 301 or 302? Justify with analytics and expiration.
\end{enumerate}
\lines{6}
\end{ex}

\simulator{A caching problem with an ID problem attached}{url-shortener}{Replace the SQL database with Cache $\to$ NoSQL and size the app tier: p99 drops to tens of milliseconds and most redirects never reach the store.}

\begin{ex}{2}{Rate limiter at the window boundary}
A limit of 10 requests per second per user. A client sends 10 requests at $t = 0.99$ s and 10 at $t = 1.01$ s.
\items
  \item How many are allowed by a fixed window (windows $[0,1)$ and $[1,2)$)? By a token bucket of capacity 10 refilling 10 tokens/s, full at $t = 0$? By a sliding log?
  \item The API has 20 gateway replicas and each keeps its own token bucket. How many requests per second can one user get? Give two fixes.
  \item One million active clients and 2 rules: memory for token buckets (100 B each) vs a sliding log at 600 requests/min per client (50 B per entry)?
  \item The counter store (Redis) is down. Fail open or fail closed for \texttt{GET /events}, and for \texttt{POST /login}?
\end{enumerate}
\lines{5}
\end{ex}

\simulator{Reject early, protect the backend}{rate-limiter}{Set a rate limit on the API Gateway below the app capacity: bots are cut first with 429, real fans keep a low p99 and the app stays below saturation.}

\begin{ex}{2}{A distributed cache for 2 M ops/s}
1 B items of 1\,000 B, plus 50 B of key and 50 B of overhead each; nodes with 64 GB of RAM serving 80\,000 ops/s; 2 M ops/s; one replica per primary.
\items
  \item Total data. How many primaries by memory, how many by throughput, and how many nodes in total?
  \item Database load at 95\% and at 90\% hit ratio. What is one point of hit ratio worth? What happens to the database when 1 of the 25 primaries dies and its keys miss?
  \item Going from 4 to 5 nodes, what fraction of keys moves with \texttt{hash mod N}, and with a consistent-hashing ring? Why add virtual nodes?
\end{enumerate}
\lines{5}
\end{ex}

\simulator{The hot partition}{sharding}{Raise the partitions to 8 and run: at t = 60 s the celebrity key still saturates one partition. Add a Cache in front of the NoSQL store for the hot reads.}

\begin{ex}{2}{News feed: push, pull or both?}
100 M daily users; each posts 0.2 times and reads the feed 10 times a day; 200 followers on average; timelines keep 800 post IDs of 8 B (overhead $\times$2); posts of 1\,000 B.
\items
  \item Posts/s and feed reads/s (peak $\times$3). What is the read/write ratio?
  \item Fan-out on write: timeline inserts per second? Timeline memory? Post storage per year?
  \item An artist with 50 M followers posts. How long would that single post take with the whole fan-out capacity of (b)?
  \item With a celebrity threshold of 10\,000 followers, 1.7\% of the authors are celebrities and 92\% of the fan-out writes disappear. Describe the hybrid read path.
\end{enumerate}
\lines{5}
\end{ex}

\simulator{Precompute the timelines}{news-feed}{Route reads to a timeline cache and writes through Kafka to fan-out workers: reads become cheap and the consumer lag shows how long a post takes to reach the timelines.}

\begin{ex}{3}{Payments that never charge twice}
BoxOffice: 300\,000 payments a day, peaks of 300/s, the provider (PSP) answers in 2 s; 0.1\% of calls are retried after a timeout.
\items
  \item Average payments/s and calls in flight at the peak. Without idempotency, how many double charges a day?
  \item The call to the PSP times out. What state does the payment go to, what do you do next, and which idempotency key do you send: \texttt{order-7f3c-attempt-1}, a random UUID per call, or \texttt{user-42}?
  \item A 200.00 BRL ticket with a 30.00 fee is charged (230.00); the PSP keeps 2.5\%. Write the ledger transaction at capture (accounts: \texttt{psp\_receivable}, \texttt{psp\_fees}, \texttt{promoter\_payable}, \texttt{platform\_revenue}; debits $+$, credits $-$). What must every transaction satisfy? After settlement (T2) and the payout to the promoter (T3), how much does the bank hold?
  \item Why not one database transaction around the PSP call? What catches what slips through?
\end{enumerate}
\lines{6}
\end{ex}

\simulator{Retries need idempotency}{payment-system}{Turn on the idempotency key on the payment edge: duplicate charges drop to zero. Then move the call behind a queue and a payment worker: buyer latency no longer depends on the provider.}

\begin{ex}{3}{BoxOffice flash sale on the whiteboard}
2 M fans arrive within 120 s of 10:00; 60\,000 seats, 2 per order; an admitted fan shops for 5 minutes; at most 10\,000 shop at once; 60\% of admitted fans buy; the PSP answers in 2 s; a hold lasts 10 minutes.
\items
  \item Joins per second; admission rate; admitted fans needed; sell-out time; orders/s; payment calls in flight.
  \item Write the SQL that holds seats without double booking (one statement, with lazy expiration). How does the service know it lost the race?
\end{enumerate}
